Components

The core controls everything else is built from. Each one adapts to the size of the view it's in, works by mouse, touch and keyboard, and uses liquid glass only where it floats over the chart. All of them live in lib/controls.js.

01

Placement and sizes

Controls float in six zones around the edge of the view, so the data keeps the middle. The layout adapts to the view's width, not the window's: switch sizes or drag the edge to watch controls collapse into icons and menus. The chart also moves its margins out from under the controls, so nothing important is ever hidden.

TLWhere you are: location and view. The view switch collapses into a menu when there's no room.
TCWhat's happening: the status island. On phones it drops to its own row so it never collides.
TRGlobal actions: search, share, appearance. On phones, search becomes an icon and the rest move into a More menu.
BLWhat you're comparing: the model picker. On phones it shows just the model colors.
BCWhen: the date stepper, always centered and in reach of a thumb. The date shortens on phones.
BRHow it's drawn: display options like the interval. Collapses to its icon on phones.
Size classes come from the viewCompact under 600px, regular to 1024px, wide above, measured on the view with container queries, so a split view or embed behaves like the device it resembles.
Edges and safe areas16px from the edge (12px on compact), plus the device's safe area so nothing sits under a notch or home indicator.
The chart steps asideThe chrome reports how much room it takes at the top and bottom, and the chart sets its margins from that.
Touch targetsOn touch screens every control grows to at least 44px, whatever the size class.
Menus become sheetsOn compact views, menus and popovers open as bottom sheets you can drag down to dismiss.
One layer at a timeOpening a menu or popover closes any other. Layers stack in a fixed order: chrome, popover, sheet, toast, tooltip.
02

Pill

The base control: a capsule of liquid glass holding a label, an icon, or both. Every other floating control is built on it.

Three sizes30, 38 (default) and 44px tall. Use 44 for primary controls; on touch screens everything is at least 44.
StatesHover brightens, press shrinks slightly with a spring, selected holds a soft fill, disabled fades to 38% and ignores input.
Long labels truncatewith an ellipsis at a set maximum width, never wrap. The full text stays available to screen readers.
CollapsingA pill marked to collapse shows only its icon on compact views, and keeps its label as an accessible name.
03

Segmented control

Picks one of a few options. The highlight slides with a spring. When the control no longer fits the room it has, it turns into a pill with a menu automatically.

KeyboardTab enters at the selected option, ← → move the selection, Home End jump to the ends.
Collapses when it doesn't fitIt measures itself against the space its zone has left. Right: the same control in a narrow spot becomes a pill and menu.
Two to five optionsShort labels. More than five, or labels that need explaining, belong in a menu.
04

Date stepper

Moves the forecast date one week at a time. It pairs with dragging the reference line on the chart itself.

Steady widthThe label is as wide as the widest date, so the arrows never shift under your finger as the date changes.
Hold to repeatPress and hold an arrow: after 400ms it repeats, speeding up. At the first or last date it nudges instead of moving.
Jump anywhereClick the date for a menu of every forecast date, opening at the current one.
Keyboard← → step, Home End go to the first or last date. Changes are announced to screen readers.
CompactThe date drops the year on narrow views, for example "Dec 27".
05

Search

One field for locations and models. Press / or ⌘K from anywhere on the page to jump to it.

Results as you typeGrouped into locations and models, with the matching part of each name in bold.
Keyboard↑ ↓ move through results, Enter picks, Esc clears, then closes. Focus never leaves the field.
CompactShows as an icon; tapping it expands the field across the top of the view.
06

Menu

A short list of choices or actions in frosted glass. It opens from its control and always stays on screen: try the controls near each corner.

Stays on screenOpens below its control, flips above when there isn't room, slides sideways at the edges, and scrolls when long.
KeyboardFocus moves into the menu. ↑ ↓, Home End, or type a letter to jump; Enter picks; Esc closes and returns focus.
Choices and actionsChoice menus show a check on the current option. Destructive actions are red and sit last.
CompactOpens as a bottom sheet with larger rows; drag it down or tap outside to dismiss.
07

Popover

For richer choices like the model picker. The pill grows into the panel and shrinks back, so it's always clear where it came from.

Grows from its controlThe pill becomes the panel and returns to it on close. With reduced motion, it simply appears.
Focus stays insideFocus moves into the popover and Tab cycles within it; Esc or a click outside closes it and returns focus to the pill.
Frosted, not liquidPanels hold text, so they use the frosted glass and never flip text color.
CompactBecomes a bottom sheet with a title and drag handle.
08

Tooltip and value tag

Tooltips name icon-only controls. Value tags write exact numbers onto the chart, next to the data they describe.

TooltipsAppear after 500ms of hover, at once on keyboard focus, and instantly when moving between controls. Never on touch: those controls need a visible label instead.
Solid, not glassTooltips usually sit over glass, and glass never sits on glass.
Value tagsMonospace numbers on a translucent chart-colored backing, median first and the interval in a quieter tone.
09

Status island and toast

The island is the one place background work shows up. Toasts confirm something you just did.

IslandGrows with a spring to show progress, then settles back. Errors stay until you act. Changes are announced to screen readers without stealing focus.
ToastAppears above the bottom controls, stays 3.2 seconds, pauses while you hover or focus it, and can carry one action like Undo.
RestraintAt most three toasts at once. Never use a toast for an error that needs fixing; that belongs in the island or next to the thing that failed.
