Design system

Foundations for the viz redesign: content that fills the screen, liquid-glass controls floating above it, and a technical tool designed with care. Every value on this page comes from tokens/source.mjs, which also generates tokens.css and tokens.json for the build.

01

Principles

What to fall back on when a decision isn't covered anywhere else. Drawn from Apple's Human Interface Guidelines, Dieter Rams, Edward Tufte, Jakob Nielsen's usability heuristics and accessibility practice, adapted for a tool that people use to judge forecasts.

1

The content is the page

Data fills the screen. Controls float above it and stay out of its way, rather than framing it with bars and panels.

2

Function first, designed all the way

Function wins every tie, but design is never an afterthought. The best answer is the one we sit with until it is both, even if it means something unconventional.

3

Honest by default

Nothing on screen should make a forecast look better, worse or more certain than it is. Scales, colors and summaries tell the truth first and look good second.

4

Clear hierarchy

Each view has one thing to look at first. Emphasis comes from position, size and weight before color, and everything that isn't the point steps back.

5

As little as possible, but no less

Every element earns its place. Remove what doesn't help someone understand or act, and keep the details that experts rely on even when they add density.

6

Consistent everywhere

The same thing looks and behaves the same way on every screen. Once someone learns a pattern, it works everywhere they meet it.

7

Act on the thing itself

Where possible, people work directly with the data rather than through menus and settings, and every action responds immediately and visibly.

8

Always say what's going on

What is selected, what is loading, how fresh the data is and what just changed are never a mystery. Status is quiet but always findable.

9

Motion explains

Things move to show where they came from and where they went. Motion is never decoration, never slows anyone down, and steps aside when the system asks for reduced motion.

10

Readable by everyone

Text keeps its contrast on any background, color is never the only signal, and everything works by keyboard and with reduced transparency or motion.

11

Glass is for controls

Liquid glass only on floating controls. Content surfaces stay solid. Never stack glass on glass.

12

One serif, rarely

Inter does the work. Instrument Serif appears once on a screen at most, for the one title that sets the place.

02

Color

Dark is pure black with neutral greys. Light is a soft grey page with a near-white chart. Both themes share every name; switch with the button in the top-right corner.

03

Type

Inter for the interface, Instrument Serif for the rare big title, Geist Mono for numbers and code. Variety comes from weight and size, not more fonts.

05

Glass

Liquid glass bends what's beneath it at the rim and magnifies it slightly through the body, with a faint color split at the edge and a rim lit from the top-left, so a control reads as a solid lens rather than a window. Drag the date lens across the lines, or focus it and use the arrow keys. Panels that hold lists use the frosted version so text stays readable.

Adapts to what's under it

Drag over the cells

The glass averages the brightness of whatever is beneath it and switches to dark or light text when the content calls for it, with a small dead zone so it never flickers. The top bar does the same as the page scrolls under it.

Three renderings

1Refract: bends a copy of the chart inside the control. Works in every browser; used for everything floating over the chart.
2Backdrop: bends whatever is behind it on the page. Chrome, Edge and Arc only; used for chrome over regular page content, like the top bar.
3Frosted: a soft blur. For panels that hold lists, for Safari and Firefox in place of backdrop, and whenever the OS asks for reduced transparency.

Rules

✓Floating controls over the chart: pills, steppers, segmented controls.
✓Frosted panels for popovers and menus that hold lists of text.
✕No glass on content: chart cards, tables and the leaderboard stay solid.
✕No glass on glass: a popover over a pill hides the pill or replaces it.
✕No large glass tiles: glass stays small enough to read as an object, not a pane.
✓Stay readable: glass switches its text to suit what's under it, and becomes frosted when the OS asks for reduced transparency.
06

Motion

Three springs cover nearly everything. They are CSS linear() curves, so plain transitions and the Web Animations API share them.
