Methodology & Evaluation Reference Guide

PART I

Probabilistic Forecasting & Benchmark Evaluation Standards

Scoring rules, reporting lags, and evaluation standards.

TEMPORAL ALIGNMENT

1. Temporal Alignment & Reporting Lags (h=0 Nowcast)

In infectious disease surveillance, operational reporting schedules introduce standard delays. Hospitals report laboratory-confirmed influenza admissions weekly to the CDC National Healthcare Safety Network (NHSN), with data published after a 5 to 10 day verification period.

Timeline Rule: On forecast reference date T (a Saturday), official hospitalization counts for the week ending on T are not yet compiled or published.
  • Reported History: Strictly cuts off at week T − 7d, representing the latest data available to a model at forecast submission time.
  • Horizon 0 (h=0): Serves as an operational nowcast of the week ending on T.
  • Horizons 1, 2, 3: Represent forward projections for T + 7d, T + 14d, and T + 21d.
  • Ground Truth Verification: Final observed counts begin at T − 7d and overlay observed values against both the nowcast and forward projections.
SCORING RULE

2. Weighted Interval Score (WIS) & Quantile Loss

FluSight models submit probabilistic forecasts across 23 standard quantile levels: τ ∈ {0.01, 0.025, 0.05, 0.10, …, 0.50, …, 0.90, 0.95, 0.975, 0.99}.

Probabilistic performance is scored using the Weighted Interval Score (WIS), mathematically equivalent to average quantile pinball loss across symmetric quantile levels (the median plus K central intervals with α_k = 2τ):

Pinball Loss: L_τ(y, q_τ) = 2 · (1(y < q_τ) − τ) · (q_τ − y)
WIS = 1/(2K + 1) · ∑_τ L_τ = 1/(K + ½) · [½|y − m| + ∑_k (α_k / 2) · IS_α_k(y)]
  • Median Point Accuracy (MAE): Absolute error between observation y and median prediction q_0.50.
  • Dispersion: Weighted widths of the prediction intervals ((α_k / 2) · (u_k − l_k)). Tighter intervals yield lower scores.
  • Miss Penalties: Linear penalties incurred when observed outcomes fall outside interval bounds, scaled by 2/α.

Fast Local Testing: Models run with --short_run produce 7 quantiles. The suite evaluates standardized WIS-7 across shared quantiles.

BENCHMARK COMPARISON

3. Pairwise Tournament Relative WIS

When comparing models that submitted across overlapping but non-identical task sets, the pairwise tournament relative WIS (Cramer et al., 2022) adjusts for differences in task coverage:

  • Pairwise Ratio: For each pair of models (m, m'), compute the ratio of mean WIS on their shared tasks: θ_{m, m'} = mean(WIS_m) ÷ mean(WIS_{m'}).
  • Geometric Mean: Compute geometric mean across all models: θ_m = (∏_{m'=1}^M θ_{m, m'})^{1/M}.
  • Baseline Scaling: Normalize against baseline: Rel WIS_m = θ_m ÷ θ_{baseline}. Values < 1.0 indicate outperformance relative to baseline. If the configured baseline has no forecasts in scope, scores are scaled to a fallback model, which is named in the leaderboard and flagged in the dataset warnings.
  • Pairwise Δ% vs Baseline: Percentage difference in mean WIS computed directly on tasks shared between a model and the reference baseline: ((WIS_m − WIS_{base}) ÷ WIS_{base}) × 100%.
EVALUATION SCOPE

4. Common Tasks Evaluation (Fair Comparison)

Epidemic intensity changes across weeks and locations. Evaluating models across different subsets of tasks can bias results when one model only forecasts during lower-incidence periods.

  • Unconstrained (Default): Scores each model on all available tasks within the current filter.
  • Common Tasks (fair=True): Restricts scoring strictly to the intersection of (reference_date, location, horizon) tuples where all selected models submitted forecasts (S = ⋂_m S_m).
GEOGRAPHIC SCALING

5. Geographic Aggregation & Population Standardization

National hospitalization counts (20,000–35,000 weekly) are orders of magnitude larger than individual state counts (20–3,000 weekly). Pooling state and national counts together without separation would allow national errors to dominate overall scores.

  • State Benchmarks (location ≠ 'US'): Evaluates state-level performance independently from national totals.
  • National Benchmarks (location == 'US'): Evaluates direct national forecasts.
  • Standardized Rate WIS (wis_per_100k): Normalizes WIS per 100,000 residents: (WIS ÷ Population) × 100,000. Not reported for targets that are already rates or proportions (e.g. % of ED visits).

When a scope includes both states and the US, every leaderboard score (WIS, Rel WIS, Δ%, coverage) is computed on the state pool only; the national value is reported separately.

CALIBRATION

6. Empirical Prediction Interval Coverage

Empirical coverage evaluates whether stated uncertainty intervals contain observed outcomes at nominal rates:

  • 95% Interval Coverage: Percentage of observations falling inside [q_0.025, q_0.975]. Nominal target is 95.0%.
  • 50% Interval Coverage: Percentage of observations falling inside [q_0.25, q_0.75] (IQR). Nominal target is 50.0%.
  • Interpretation: Coverage below nominal rates indicates overconfidence (prediction intervals too narrow); coverage above nominal rates indicates underconfidence (prediction intervals wider than necessary).
  • 95% Interval Width (leaderboard "Sharpness" column): Average width q_0.975 − q_0.025. Narrower intervals are preferred only when nominal coverage is maintained.
ERROR DECOMPOSITION

7. WIS Error Decomposition (Bracher et al., 2021)

The Weighted Interval Score decomposes additively into three components, each sharing the WIS normalization 1/(K + ½):

WIS(F, y) = 1/(K + ½) · [Dispersion + Overprediction + Underprediction]
  • Dispersion (Sharpness component): ∑ (α_k / 2) · (u_k − l_k). Measures the intrinsic uncertainty spread of the forecast distribution.
  • Overprediction Penalty: ∑ (l_k − y) · 1(y < l_k) + 0.5 · (m − y) · 1(y < m). Incurred when observed outcomes fall below lower forecast quantiles or below the median.
  • Underprediction Penalty: ∑ (y − u_k) · 1(y > u_k) + 0.5 · (y − m) · 1(y > m). Incurred when observed outcomes exceed upper forecast quantiles or exceed the median.
Sample Size Note: Evaluating error decompositions over small sample sizes (N ≤ 4 horizons on a single date and location) exhibits high variance. Multi-date or multi-location aggregations provide more reliable estimates.
MODEL REGISTRY & STAGING

8. Model Classification & Sandbox Staging

The sandbox evaluates models across source tiers and designations:

  • [REPO] Production Models: Stored in model-output/ and tracked in version control.
  • [LOCAL] Sandbox Staging: Development runs placed in local-output/ (ignored by git). Evaluated side-by-side with repository models.
  • Model Designations: Classified as Baseline (reference benchmark), Primary (designated hub team model), or Component (standalone ensemble contributor).
SCALE TRANSFORMATION

9. Log-Scale Scoring & Transformed Evaluation (ln(y + 1))

Epidemic counts vary widely across jurisdictions of differing population size and between epidemic wave peaks and troughs. Absolute scale metrics like standard WIS can be dominated by high-incidence peaks in large states (e.g., California or Texas), obscuring predictive skill in smaller jurisdictions.

  • Mathematical Transformation: Follows standard Hubverse methodology (Bosse et al., 2023) by transforming observations and forecast quantiles: y_{log} = \ln(\max(y, 0) + 1) and q_{log} = \ln(\max(q, 0) + 1).
  • Log WIS & MAE: Pinball loss and median absolute errors are evaluated directly on the transformed scale, ensuring multiplicative percentage deviations are weighted equitably across both high- and low-transmission environments.
  • Log Tournament Relative WIS: Pairwise tournament ratios and baseline normalization (Rel WIS < 1.0 = outperformance) are computed independently on the log scale, providing a standardized evaluation that balances small-state and peak-surge accuracy.
SYNTHETIC ENSEMBLES

10. Synthetic Ensemble Studio & Quantile Vincentization

The suite features a built-in Synthetic Ensemble Studio allowing researchers to construct, test, and persist on-the-fly custom ensembles without modifying upstream code:

  • Vincentization: Averages predictive quantiles across constituent models per forecast task: q_{ens}(\alpha) = \text{Aggregate}(q_1(\alpha), ..., q_M(\alpha)) using either the median or arithmetic mean across models.
  • Quantile Rearrangement: Enforces strict monotonicity across all 23 quantile levels within each forecast task, ensuring predictions never cross (q_{\alpha_1} \le q_{\alpha_2} for \alpha_1 < \alpha_2).
  • Spatial Split Routing: Permits separate constituent model pools for State jurisdictions (50 states + DC + PR) versus US National aggregate forecasts, matching operational architectures of top Hubverse ensembles such as UMass-flusion_spatial2_prod.
EVALUATION SCOPE

11. Evaluation Windows, Season Phases & Weekly Wins

The leaderboard's TIME control switches between a single reference Date and a Season window. The window menu combines any set of seasons, a week range dragged across the mini season chart, and a phase preset. The window applies to the forecast (target) week: a task is scored when the week being forecast falls inside it.

  • Week range: season weeks are aligned across seasons (week 1 = MMWR week 31, early August), so "mid-November to early February" means the same stretch of every selected season.
  • Peak: for each location and season, the contiguous run of weeks around the season maximum where observed values stay at or above 60% of that maximum (typically 3–6 weeks).
  • Pre-peak / Post-peak: the weeks before and after that location's peak. A season whose highest week is still among its latest observations has not peaked yet, so all of its weeks count as pre-peak.
  • Per location: with All States, every state is scored on its own peak, not the national one. The mini chart then shows the national curve for orientation.
  • Wins: for each round in scope, the model with the lowest mean WIS on the tasks every selected model forecast that week wins it (ties count for each tied model), shown as won/competed. A model can lead on total WIS through a few large weeks while rarely being the best week to week; Wins shows that. It depends on which models are selected.
SEASON PEAK

12. Season Peak Forecasts: Timing, Size & Scoring

Hubs such as FluSight pair weekly hospital admissions with two season-level targets that have no horizon: peak week inc flu hosp, a probability for each candidate week of being the season's highest (a pmf over Saturdays), and peak inc flu hosp, quantiles of that highest weekly count. They are stored apart from the weekly forecasts, so the fan chart, leaderboard, heatmap and diagnostics are unchanged by them.

  • Summaries: the peak-week median and central 50% / 90% windows are read off the cumulative probabilities (the 50% window runs from the week where it first reaches 25% to where it reaches 75%). A pmf that does not sum exactly to 1 is renormalized and flagged in the hub's data-quality list. Peak size uses the submitted quantiles directly.
  • Where they appear: the Season Peak view (the Outlook's target-zone boxes and peak-week ridgelines, Peak Call by Round, and the Scorecard); the Peak Forecasts signal on the fan chart (▲ at both medians, a 50% × 50% box, 90% whiskers); and the spatial map's Peak Week and Peak Size layers.
  • Observed peak: the highest reported week inside the forecast window, using the same merged truth as the rest of the dashboard. It is provisional (◇ peak so far) until observations reach the window's last candidate week, then final (★). Peak scores are only computed for final peaks.
  • Timing scores: the ranked probability score RPS = Σ (F(w) − 1[w ≥ observed])² over ordered weeks, in weeks (a forecast certain of one week scores its absolute error, so near misses are rewarded); the log score −ln P(observed week), floored at P = 0.001 so one confident miss cannot dominate an average; and the signed error of the median week. Weeks tied at the maximum all count for the log score; the earliest is used for RPS and the median error.
  • Size scores: the leaderboard's WIS, reported as a percentage of the observed peak so states can be pooled, plus the median's percentage error and 50% / 90% interval coverage.
  • Chance the peak is past: the probability a forecast put on weeks up to its own data cutoff, that is, weeks that were already observed when it was made.
PART II

Spatial View & Epidemic Wave Tracking

Geographic normalization, wave trajectories, prediction corridors, and aggregation.

POPULATION NORMALIZATION

11. Spatial Normalization & Per-Capita Rates (Rate / 100k)

Raw patient admission counts scale with population size. The spatial dashboard supports two viewing modes:

Incidence Rate: R_s = (Projected Admissions_s ÷ Census Population_s) × 100,000
  • Rate / 100k: Standardizes admissions by population to reflect relative epidemic intensity across states.
  • Admissions: Raw expected patient volume for resource and capacity planning.
WAVE TRAJECTORY

12. Horizon Dynamics & Trajectory Phases

Spatial trajectory indicates projected direction and magnitude relative to the reference week:

  • Horizon 0 (h=0) — Nowcast Baseline: Represents the reference week ending on Saturday T (Δ% = 0.0% by definition).
  • Horizons 1, 2, 3: Forward projections for 1, 2, and 3 weeks ahead (T + 7d, T + 14d, T + 21d).
Growth: Δ% = ((Median_h − Median_h0) ÷ Median_h0) × 100%
  • Rising (> +5%): Increasing admissions trajectory.
  • Plateau (−5% to +5%): Stable trajectory near peak or steady state.
  • Receding (< −5%): Declining admissions trajectory.
CALIBRATION

13. State-Level Prediction Corridors & WIS

When selecting any state on the map, the dashboard evaluates state-specific probabilistic performance:

  • 95% Prediction Corridor: The interval bounded by [q_0.025, q_0.975].
    • Within 95% PI: Observed value falls inside the interval.
    • Interval Miss: Observed value falls outside the interval (underprediction if truth > q_0.975; overprediction if truth < q_0.025).
  • State WIS: Quantile score evaluated specifically for the selected state.
SPATIAL COHERENCE

14. Bottom-Up State Sums vs. Direct National Models

Models in FluSight produce both individual state forecasts and an independent national forecast for the United States.

Quantile Aggregation: Quantiles are not additive: the median of a sum generally differs from the sum of medians ∑ Median_s. The two coincide when every state distribution (and hence the sum) is symmetric; correlation between states changes the spread of the national distribution, not this identity.

The direct national forecast Median_US is also a separately fitted model output, so it can differ from any bottom-up aggregate even when quantile arithmetic is not a factor.

Comparing the bottom-up sum against direct national models provides insight into spatial coherence across model outputs.

SPATIAL METRICS

15. Spatial Summary Statistics

Summary indicators computed across state-level projections:

  • Highest Incidence State: Jurisdiction with max(Rate per 100k).
  • Fastest Surge: Jurisdiction with max(Δ% Growth).
  • Wave Spread Status: Proportion of jurisdictions with growing trajectories (> +5%) versus receding trajectories (< −5%).
PART III

Surveillance Signals, Technical Indicators & Display Modes

Surveillance stream definitions, smoothing filters, dynamic indicators, and visual modes.

SURVEILLANCE INTEGRATION

16. Surveillance Data Streams: NSSP, ILINet & FluSurv-NET

The visualizer incorporates multi-source surveillance data alongside forecast targets:

  • NHSN Hospital Admissions: Weekly laboratory-confirmed influenza admissions (wk inc flu hosp), serving as the primary evaluation target.
  • NSSP Flu Emergency Department (% ED Visits): CDC National Syndromic Surveillance Program data reporting the percentage of emergency department visits diagnosed with influenza. This signal typically leads hospital admissions by 1 to 2 weeks.
  • ILINet (Scaled ILI+): Outpatient Influenza-like Illness Surveillance Network data multiplied by laboratory test positivity percent (wILI × % positive / 100), aligned by MMWR week across seasons 2010–2023.
  • FluSurv-NET Laboratory Rate (Rate / 100k): Population-based laboratory-confirmed hospitalizations per 100,000 residents across 14 network states (2010–2023).
TREND SMOOTHING

17. Trend Smoothing Filters: MA-2, MA-4 & EMA-3

Filters reduce weekly reporting noise and holiday-related data irregularities:

  • 2-Week Moving Average (MA-2): MA_2(t) = (y_t + y_{t-1}) / 2. Fast 2-point smoothing with minimal phase lag.
  • 4-Week Moving Average (MA-4): MA_4(t) = 0.25 · ∑_{i=0}^3 y_{t-i}. Four-week smoothing representing monthly trend.
  • 3-Week Exponential Moving Average (EMA-3): EMA_t = α y_t + (1 − α) EMA_{t-1} with smoothing weight α = 2 / (3 + 1) = 0.50. Weights recent data more heavily while attenuating high-frequency noise.
EPIDEMIC DYNAMICS

18. Epidemic Velocity & Week-over-Week Growth

Rate-of-change indicators provide leading signals before absolute volume peaks:

  • Net Case Velocity (WoW Velocity): First difference v_t = y_t − y_{t-1} (net admissions per week). Positive values indicate expanding admissions; negative values indicate contraction.
  • Week-over-Week Growth Rate (% WoW): Relative growth g_t = ((y_t − y_{t-1}) / y_{t-1}) × 100%. Reflects relative acceleration or deceleration of the epidemic curve.
HISTORICAL BENCHMARKS

19. Aligned Historical Seasons

Enables overlaying historical seasons ('23–'24, '22–'23, '21–'22, and '20–'21) aligned week-for-week against the active forecast horizon.

  • MMWR Epiweek Alignment: Aligned by CDC MMWR season_week (Week 1 through 52/53) to preserve calendar alignment for Thanksgiving, Christmas, and New Year holidays across seasons.
  • Rendering Modes: Toggle between monochrome recency gradient (older seasons rendered with higher opacity) and chromatic tints (distinct colors per season).
REFERENCE BOUNDS

20. Season Peak Level Reference

Draws a horizontal reference line at the highest weekly admissions count observed to date in the current flu season, providing context on whether model projections anticipate new seasonal peaks or post-peak declines.

DISPLAY MODES

21. Display Modes: Dual Y-Axis vs. Normalized (0–100%)

Supports two display modes for comparing surveillance streams with differing units:

  • Dual Y-Axis Mode (Right Axis): Displays hospital admissions on the primary left axis and secondary streams (% ED visits, rate per 100k, velocity) on the right axis (yaxis2), preserving absolute values.
  • Normalized Mode (0–100% of Peak): Rescales admissions, forecast quantiles, and active surveillance streams to v_{norm} = (v / v_{max}) × 100%, enabling direct visual comparison of relative timing and peaks. Velocity and growth keep their own right axis.
  • Axis Scaling: Every axis always contains every value drawn on it in the visible window (truth, medians, each displayed prediction interval, width rulers, indicators, and the season-peak line), so nothing is clipped. When stepping between dates, the previous scale is held while it is at most 1.5× looser than needed and otherwise eases halfway toward the fitted scale, so the axis does not jump after a wide peak-week forecast.
  • Interval Width Rulers: Optional solid bars beside the fan that redraw each model's last-horizon prediction intervals side by side (thin = 95%, medium = 80%, thick = 50%), so interval widths can be compared directly.
PART IV

Heatmap Matrix Analytics & Cross-Sectional Surveillance

Multi-jurisdiction wave progression, peak-timing sorting, and cross-model performance matrices.

MATRIX ARCHITECTURE

22. Cross-Sectional Matrix Principles & Analytical Presets

The Heatmap Matrix view complements the single-location Timeline Fan and single-week Spatial Choropleth by projecting high-density multidimensional surveillance and forecast data onto a 2D cross-sectional grid:

  • Spatio-Temporal Wave Matrix (Location × Date): Renders nationwide temporal progression across all 52 jurisdictions over the entire season.
  • Forward Outlook Matrix (Location × Horizon): Evaluates near-term trajectories (h=0 to h=3) across states for operational readiness.
  • Model Breakdown Matrix (Model × Location): Identifies localized modeling strengths and regional biases across models.
  • Horizon Error Decay Matrix (Model × Horizon): Quantifies how uncertainty and interval error accumulate as forecast lead time extends.
  • Error & Shock Timeline Matrix (Model × Date): Detects reporting anomalies, holiday backlog artifacts, and systemic turning-point errors.
WAVE RECONSTRUCTION

23. Peak-Timing Sorting Algorithm & Epidemic Wavefronts

Standard alphabetical ordering hides physical epidemiological propagation. The Peak-Timing Sort reorganizes geographic jurisdictions along the Y-axis chronologically according to the epidemiological week of their maximum observed incidence:

Argmax Ordering: For jurisdiction i, compute t^*_i = \arg\max_{t} v_{i,t}. Sort rows by (t^*_i, -\max_t v_{i,t}).

This mathematical reconstruction aligns the diagonal wavefront across the matrix, exposing transmission corridors (e.g. initial southern surges followed weeks later by midwestern and northeastern peaks).

NORMALIZATION MATH

24. Row-Relative Min-Max Peak Scaling

Due to population scale and healthcare capacity disparities, absolute metrics can obscure timing in smaller states. The Row Min-Max Normalization rescales each state's time series onto a unit interval [0.0, 1.0]:

v'_{i,t} = \frac{v_{i,t} - \min_t(v_{i,t})}{\max_t(v_{i,t}) - \min_t(v_{i,t})}

Where 1.0 represents that jurisdiction's localized seasonal crest and 0.0 represents its baseline nadir. This allows epidemiologists to compare wave shape, crest duration, and lead-lag dynamics independent of baseline population magnitude.
